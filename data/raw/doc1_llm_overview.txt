Large Language Models: Foundations and Advances

Large language models (LLMs) have fundamentally transformed natural language processing (NLP) over the past decade.
Models such as GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, and LLaMA have demonstrated unprecedented capabilities in text generation,
reasoning, summarization, and code synthesis.

At the core of these systems is the Transformer architecture, introduced by Vaswani et al. in 2017.
The Transformer relies on a self-attention mechanism that allows each token in a sequence to attend to every
other token, enabling the model to capture long-range dependencies without recurrence.

Pretraining on vast corpora of internet text using next-token prediction has proven to be a powerful
unsupervised learning objective. OpenAI's GPT series scaled this approach from 117M to over 1 trillion
parameters. Google DeepMind's Gemini models are trained on multimodal data including text, code, images,
and audio, blurring the line between language and vision models.

Instruction tuning and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) are now standard post-training steps.
RLHF, pioneered by Anthropic and OpenAI, uses human preference data to align model outputs with human intent,
reducing harmful, biased, or unhelpful responses.

Key challenges remain: hallucination (generating factually incorrect but plausible-sounding content),
context length limitations, computational cost, and interpretability. Researchers at DeepMind, Meta AI,
Microsoft Research, and Stanford are actively working on solutions such as retrieval-augmented generation (RAG),
chain-of-thought prompting, and sparse attention mechanisms.

The field is moving rapidly. By 2025, open-source models such as LLaMA 3 and Mistral have closed much of
the gap with proprietary systems, enabling broader research access and deployment flexibility.
