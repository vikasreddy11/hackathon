Knowledge Graphs in AI: Structure, Construction, and Applications

A knowledge graph (KG) is a structured representation of real-world entities and the relationships between them.
Nodes represent entities (people, organizations, concepts, places) and edges represent typed relationships
(e.g., WORKS_AT, FOUNDED_BY, LOCATED_IN, PART_OF).

Major knowledge graphs in production include Wikidata (maintained by the Wikimedia Foundation), Google's
Knowledge Graph (powering search features), Freebase (acquired by Google in 2010), and DBpedia (extracted
from Wikipedia). In the biomedical domain, ontologies such as SNOMED CT, Gene Ontology, and DrugBank serve
as domain-specific knowledge graphs.

Knowledge graph construction pipelines typically involve:
- Named Entity Recognition (NER): Identifying entity mentions in text using models like spaCy, BERT-NER,
  or GLiNER. Common entity types include PERSON, ORGANIZATION, GPE (geopolitical entity), PRODUCT, and EVENT.
- Relation Extraction (RE): Classifying the semantic relationship between entity pairs. Models include
  REBEL (from Babelscape), GenIE, and PL-Marker.
- Entity Linking (EL): Mapping extracted entity mentions to canonical entries in a reference KB such as
  Wikidata or UMLS.
- Coreference Resolution: Resolving pronoun and mention chains to ensure consistent entity identity.

Graph databases such as Neo4j, Amazon Neptune, and TigerGraph provide efficient storage and traversal of
large knowledge graphs. Query languages like SPARQL (for RDF graphs) and Cypher (for Neo4j property graphs)
enable expressive graph queries.

Knowledge graphs enhance LLM pipelines through structured grounding, enabling multi-hop reasoning that is
difficult to achieve with flat document retrieval alone. Systems like KGRAG, ToG (Think-on-Graph), and
KGP (Knowledge Graph Prompting) demonstrate significant accuracy gains on complex QA benchmarks.
