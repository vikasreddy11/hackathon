Evaluation of AI Systems: Benchmarks, Metrics, and Best Practices

Rigorous evaluation is critical for understanding the capabilities and limitations of AI systems.
The field has developed a rich ecosystem of benchmarks, automated metrics, and human evaluation protocols.

Standard NLP benchmarks include:
- GLUE and SuperGLUE: Multi-task language understanding benchmarks covering sentiment, inference,
  coreference, and QA tasks. Introduced by Wang et al. at NYU and Google.
- MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding): Tests knowledge across 57 academic subjects.
  State-of-the-art models like GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra achieve >80% accuracy.
- HumanEval: Code generation benchmark from OpenAI, measuring functional correctness of generated programs.
- BIG-Bench: A collaborative benchmark of 204 tasks designed to probe LLM limitations.
- HELM (Holistic Evaluation of Language Models): Stanford's framework covering accuracy, calibration,
  robustness, fairness, and efficiency.

For RAG-specific evaluation, the RAGAS framework (Retrieval-Augmented Generation Assessment) measures:
- Faithfulness: Are claims in the answer supported by retrieved context?
- Answer Relevancy: Does the answer address the question?
- Context Precision and Recall: Quality of retrieved chunks relative to the ground truth.

Knowledge graph quality metrics include triple precision/recall, entity linking accuracy, and
relation extraction F1 score. Tools like KEEN (Knowledge Graph Embedding Evaluation) benchmark
link prediction and entity disambiguation.

Human evaluation remains the gold standard for open-ended generation tasks. Evaluation dimensions include
fluency, coherence, factual accuracy, helpfulness, and harmlessness. Companies like Scale AI and Surge AI
provide crowdsourcing platforms for large-scale human annotation.

Responsible AI evaluation increasingly includes bias audits (using datasets like BBQ and WinoBias),
safety red-teaming (structured adversarial prompting), and privacy assessments. Regulatory frameworks
such as the EU AI Act are beginning to mandate evaluation transparency for high-risk AI systems.
